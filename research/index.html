Research

Applied Research for Digital Adoption

We study how Canadian small businesses, newcomers, and nonprofits actually adopt technology — then we turn those findings into better programs, open-source tools, and practical guidance that the entire ecosystem can use.

Current Research Focus

Our research agenda is driven by the challenges we see on the ground every day. These are the questions we are actively investigating in partnership with community organizations across British Columbia.

Odoo Inventory Management for Small Businesses

Enterprise resource planning tools like Odoo are powerful but notoriously difficult for small teams to implement. We are documenting real-world implementation patterns, common failure points, and step-by-step onboarding guides specifically for businesses with fewer than 20 employees. Our goal is to produce open-access playbooks that reduce the time and cost of ERP adoption by at least 40%.

Cloud Adoption Patterns

Many small businesses in Canada still rely on paper records, desktop spreadsheets, or legacy on-premise software. We are mapping the decision-making process these businesses go through when considering cloud services — from Google Workspace and QuickBooks Online to industry-specific SaaS platforms. Our research tracks barriers including cost perception, data privacy concerns, and the digital literacy gap.

Digital Literacy Assessment

There is no widely accepted framework for measuring digital literacy among Canadian small business owners. We are developing and validating an assessment tool that captures not just technical skills but also digital confidence, information evaluation abilities, and awareness of cybersecurity fundamentals. This tool will be freely available for use by other organizations serving similar communities.

Our Methodology

Good research starts with good values. These principles guide every study we undertake, ensuring our work is both rigorous and genuinely useful to the communities we serve.

01

Community-Centered

Research participants are not data points — they are collaborators. We co-design studies with the communities we serve, ensuring that research questions reflect real needs rather than academic curiosity. Participants review findings before publication and have a voice in how the research is applied. This approach draws on participatory action research traditions and is central to how TechMind operates.

02

Open Access

All of our research outputs — reports, datasets, assessment tools, and implementation guides — are published under open licenses. We believe that publicly funded research should be publicly available, especially when it concerns the digital well-being of underserved communities. Our work is freely accessible on GitHub and through our website with no paywalls or registration requirements.

03

Actionable Insights

We measure the success of our research by whether it changes practice, not just by citation counts. Every study we publish includes a clear "so what" section with practical recommendations for small business owners, service providers, and policymakers. Our Odoo implementation research, for example, includes ready-to-use configuration templates and training curricula alongside the academic analysis.

Open-Source on GitHub

All TechMind research publications, datasets, assessment tools, and implementation guides are published on our GitHub organization. We use open licenses (Creative Commons and MIT) so that other charities, educators, and community organizations can freely adapt our work for their own contexts. If you find something useful, fork it. If you improve it, send us a pull request. Research is better when it is built in the open.

Interested in Collaborating on Research?

We partner with universities, nonprofits, government agencies, and industry organizations across Canada. If you are working on digital adoption, digital literacy, or technology education research, we would love to explore how we can work together.
